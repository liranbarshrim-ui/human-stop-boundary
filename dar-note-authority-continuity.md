# When Authority Dies: Continuity Under Sudden Absence

*DAR Internal Research Note — May 2026*

---

## The Gap This Note Addresses

DAR asks a simple governance question:
Who, by name, is authorized to refuse continuation before a decision becomes irreversible?

The question assumes a name exists.

This note asks a different question: what happens to that authority when the person holding it disappears suddenly?

It is not a hypothetical. It is a structural test that every governance document either passes or fails — visibly, in writing, in advance.

---

## What Triggered This Note

A small listed company lost its Chief Risk Officer to sudden death. He had held the role for over a decade. He was the named actor in the company's risk oversight chain: the person through whom risk management flowed to the Audit Committee and the Board.

The company had no formal risk management committee. The governance structure was: Board, Audit Committee, and with the assistance of the CRO. Not a function. A person.

After his death, the company issued a statement confirming that board composition remained compliant.

That statement answered a compliance question.

It did not answer a governance question.

---

## The Distinction

Compliance asks: does the role exist.

DAR asks: does the authority survive the person who held it.

These are not the same question.

*The reports named a person.*
*But a name is not a mechanism.*

When the person disappeared, continuity remained undocumented.

---

## The Authority Continuity Test

The standard DAR question is:

Who, by name, is explicitly authorized to refuse continuation before a decision becomes irreversible?

This case introduces a second test:

If that person disappeared tomorrow — without warning — where would refusal authority live?

Three possible answers:

**In the person.** Not written anywhere. Not transferable. Disappears with them.

**In the role.** Attached to the title, not the individual. Survives — but only if the authority was actually scoped and documented as belonging to the role.

**In the documented structure.** Named, bounded, with an explicit continuity mechanism. This is what survives. This is what DAR requires.

The test is simple:

If this person were gone tomorrow, would a successor inherit the authority — or only the title?

---

## Why This Is Not a Failure Case

There is no evidence of risk failure in the company described above. There is no indication that decisions became orphaned or that continuity was actually broken.

That is precisely the point.

The gap is not visible in outcomes. It is visible only in documentation.

DAR does not prevent death. It prevents the situation where a person's death is also the death of the authority they carried.

---

## The Formulation

*The reports named a person.*
*But a name is not a mechanism.*
*When the person disappeared, continuity remained undocumented.*

A named authority is not yet an institutional authority.
The appointment is the beginning of the answer.
Continuity is the rest of it.
