Human Oversight

Human-in-the-loop is not the same as refusal authority.

Most modern governance systems already include oversight layers: review committees, approval chains, human validation, monitoring systems, compliance controls, and escalation procedures.

Yet irreversible continuation still happens without meaningful interruption.

A human inside the loop does not guarantee that a human can stop the loop.

Structural Difference

DAR examines a different question.

Most governance systems examine execution responsibility: who reviewed, who approved, who monitored, or who complied with procedure.

Decision Authority Research examines whether a specific individual is operationally authorized to refuse irreversible continuation before execution proceeds.

DAR defines refusal authority.

Core Observation

The missing layer is often not visibility.

Organizations usually know that execution is happening. The deeper problem is that interruption carries institutional, political, financial, or operational cost.

As systems scale, continuation becomes structurally easier than refusal.

Under those conditions, “human oversight” can quietly become observational rather than sovereign.

A safeguard only becomes real when someone can refuse continuation without ambiguity.

Decision Authority Research

The central DAR question.

Before irreversible execution proceeds under uncertainty: who, by name, is authorized to stop continuation?
