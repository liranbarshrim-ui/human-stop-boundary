Trigger Authority
Who Defined the Threshold?
Observational Research Only · June 2026 · 23 cases reviewed
Core Question

DAR asks:

Who, by name, is authorized to refuse?

A prior question may exist:

Who defined in advance when refusal becomes necessary?

This document refers to that condition as Trigger Authority.

Proposed Definition
Trigger Authority
A named role, function, regulation, or operational mechanism that defines in advance the conditions under which interruption, review, refusal, or release becomes required.

The question is not: "Who stopped?"

The question is: "Who defined the threshold that makes stopping relevant?"

Coding Method

Three possible classifications:

Yes
A threshold, condition, event, or trigger requiring interruption was defined in advance.
No
No threshold was defined in advance. Recognition occurred during or after the event.
?
Public evidence does not allow determination.

Observations
Observation 1
Trigger Authority is not equivalent to Effective Authority.
Toyota Andon  ·  Trigger Yes  ·  Effective Yes
Challenger  ·  Trigger Yes  ·  Effective No
GDPR DPO  ·  Trigger Yes  ·  Effective No

The trigger existed. The interruption did not.
Observation 2
Some failures appear to occur before refusal authority becomes relevant.
Boeing MCAS  ·  Trigger No  ·  Effective No
Wells Fargo  ·  Trigger No  ·  Effective No
VW Dieselgate  ·  Trigger No  ·  Effective No
Knight Capital  ·  Trigger No  ·  Effective No

The problem may not be "who could stop?" but "no threshold was defined that would require stopping."
Observation 3
The strongest observed interruption structures contain both Trigger Authority and Effective Authority.
Toyota Andon  ·  Trigger Yes  ·  Effective Yes
US Nuclear PAL  ·  Trigger Yes  ·  Effective Yes
EU Qualified Person  ·  Trigger Yes  ·  Effective Yes
NRC Shift Supervisor  ·  Trigger Yes  ·  Effective Yes

Whether this relationship is causal remains unknown.

What This Does Not Claim
  • Trigger Authority prevents failure.
  • Trigger Authority improves outcomes.
  • Trigger Authority predicts success.
  • Trigger Authority eliminates judgment errors or shared assumption failures.
  • A system satisfying all three conditions will not fail.

A system may satisfy Trigger Authority, Stop Authority, and Effective Authority and still fail. DAR identifies authority structures, not the correctness of beliefs or the quality of decisions.

Working Hypothesis
Many governance failures begin one layer earlier than refusal authority.

The question may not be: "Who was authorized to refuse?"

The prior question may be: "Who defined the threshold that made refusal necessary?"

If no threshold exists, refusal authority may never become operational.
