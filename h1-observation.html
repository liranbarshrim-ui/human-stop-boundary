H1
Preliminary Observation: Effective Authority and Post-Event Authority Ambiguity
Observed association across 23 cases
Observational only · Not causal · No inter-rater validation · Further validation required
Why This Question
A control point can influence a decision. Effective Authority can prevent continuation.

If that distinction is real, it should produce an observable difference: where continuation can be stopped, disagreement about who was supposed to stop it should be lower after the event.

This observation tests that expectation against 23 cases.

The Question

This review examined 23 cases across manufacturing, aviation, finance, healthcare, pharmaceuticals, government, and AI systems.

The question was intentionally narrow:

When a consequential event occurs, does disagreement emerge regarding who was expected to interrupt, refuse, or stop continuation?

Cases were coded for two variables: Effective Authority and Post-Event Authority Ambiguity.

Method (Preliminary)
Coding procedure
  • Cases were drawn from the DAR Research Matrix v3.
  • Coding was performed manually using publicly available case records, investigation reports, regulatory findings, court records, and governance documentation where available.
  • Post-Event Authority Ambiguity was coded as No, Partial, or Yes based on the observed degree of public disagreement regarding interruption authority after the event.
  • No inter-rater validation was performed.
  • This document records an observational coding exercise, not a statistical study.
Coding Definitions

Effective Authority

YesContinuation cannot proceed against named refusal.
PartialVeto exists but override path remains open.
NoContinuation proceeds regardless of objection.

Post-Event Authority Ambiguity

NoAuthority was clear; minimal public dispute after the event.
PartialGeneral agreement on authority; dispute on execution or escalation.
YesSignificant public dispute on who was expected to stop.

Summary — Observed Distribution
Effective = Yes  →  5/5 coded No ambiguity
Effective = Partial  →  4/5 coded Partial or Yes ambiguity
Effective = No  →  11/11 coded Partial or Yes ambiguity (excluding Knight Capital and Flash Crash — epistemic failures, not authority failures)
Three Observed Patterns
Pattern 1
Where Effective Authority appears operationally independent, post-event authority ambiguity appears low.
In these cases, disagreement about who could interrupt continuation was limited or absent after the event.
Toyota Andon · US Nuclear PAL · EU Qualified Person · Aviation PIC · NRC Shift Supervisor
Pattern 2
Where authority exists but remains conditionally overridable, ambiguity appears reduced but not eliminated.
Questions regarding escalation, override, and responsibility remain present in the public record.
CRO Veto · NHS Caldicott Guardian · US Responsible Official · ISO 27001 CISO
Pattern 3
Where Effective Authority is absent, post-event authority ambiguity frequently appears — except where the primary failure mode is epistemic rather than authority-related.
Public discussion repeatedly shifts toward questions such as: Who knew? Who could have stopped this? Who was responsible for interruption?

In epistemic failures (Knight Capital, Flash Crash), disagreement centers on information, detection, or situational awareness rather than interruption authority. Ambiguity about who should have stopped is low — because the failure preceded any moment where authority could have acted.
Challenger · Wells Fargo · VW Dieselgate · Boeing MCAS · SOX CFO · LUNA / Terra
Epistemic exceptions: Knight Capital · Flash Crash
Important Limitations
What this observation does not claim
  • This observation does not demonstrate causation.
  • It does not show that Effective Authority prevents failure.
  • It does not show that Effective Authority improves outcomes.
  • Coding reflects single-reviewer judgment with no inter-rater validation.
  • The sample is not random. Cases were selected for the DAR Research Matrix, not for statistical representativeness.
  • It only suggests that the presence or absence of Effective Authority may be associated with different levels of post-event authority ambiguity.
Working Hypothesis
H1
Cases with Effective Authority appear to exhibit lower post-event authority ambiguity than cases without Effective Authority.
Preliminary observation across 23 cases. Consistent with the current coding. Further validation required.
