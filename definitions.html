Definitions

Core Terms

Most organizations define approval paths. Far fewer define who can stop momentum before exposure becomes irreversible.

If no one is clearly named to stop it, the gap already exists.
01

Named Stop Authority

A single identified person formally empowered to stop a specific irreversible decision.

Not a team. Not a role. A person.
02

Decision Authority Record (DAR)

A written record naming who holds stop authority before execution begins.

Clarity before exposure.
03

Irreversible Decision

A decision that cannot be undone without disproportionate cost, harm, or loss.

Threshold must be recognized early.
04

Stop Authority Gap

No clearly named person can stop the decision when needed.

Momentum rules by default.
05

Escalation Window

A defined response period granted to the named authority once a stop issue appears.

Example: 24 hours.
06

Replacement Authority

If no action is taken within the escalation window, authority transfers automatically.

No paralysis. No symbolic ownership.
07

Oversight Trigger

Any use of stop authority, refusal to act, or expiry of the window creates automatic review.

Verification, not punishment.
08

Frozen Authority

Authority exists formally, but no timely action is taken while exposure grows.

Delay becomes decision.
09

Distributed Permission

Many can approve continuation, yet no single person can stop.

Everyone approves. Nobody owns.
10

Decision Exposure

The legal, financial, reputational, or personal consequences attached to a moving decision.

Risk always lands somewhere.
