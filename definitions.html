Definitions

Core Terms

These are not standard corporate terms.

They describe recurring governance failures that existing language often avoids naming clearly.

Framework Language for Real-World Decision Exposure
01
Named Stop Authority

A single identified person formally empowered to stop a specific irreversible decision.

Named before execution begins.

Not a team. Not a role. A person.
02
Irreversible Decision

A decision that cannot be undone without disproportionate cost, harm, or loss.

The threshold should be recognized before it is crossed.

Late awareness is structurally useless.
03
Decision Authority Record (DAR)

A written record identifying who holds stop authority over a decision pathway.

Created before execution begins.

DAR names who can stop.
04
Escalation Window

A defined response period granted to the named authority once a stop issue appears.

If unused, authority transfers automatically.

Example: 24 hours.
05
Alternate Authority

A predefined successor receives authority if the primary holder fails to act.

No control should depend on one unavailable person.

Redundancy prevents paralysis.
06
Oversight Trigger

Use of stop authority, refusal to act, or expiry of the response window creates an automatic review event.

The purpose is verification, not punishment.

Every stop event should leave an audit trail.
If no one can clearly say who may stop it, the gap already exists.

Most frameworks explain approval.

Far fewer define who must stop continuation when risk rises.

DAR begins there.
