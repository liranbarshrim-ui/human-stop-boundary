Definitions

Core Terms

Most organizations define approval paths. Almost none define who holds the institutional right to refuse continuation before execution becomes irreversible.

The absence of named refusal authority does not mean continuation is authorized.
It means authorization was never required.
01

Refusal Authority

The institutional right, assigned by name in advance, to deny continuation before irreversible execution proceeds.

Not a team. Not a role. A person. Named before execution begins.
02

Decision Authority Record (DAR)

A governance constraint requiring named refusal authority before irreversible execution proceeds. Documents who holds that authority, under what conditions, and who may authorize continuation.

Legitimacy before execution. Not documentation after.
03

Irreversible Execution

The transition from approved capability to live action that cannot be undone without disproportionate cost, harm, or loss.

The threshold must be recognized before it is crossed.
04

Legitimacy Gap

The condition in which irreversible execution proceeds without named authorization. No one holds the institutional right to refuse continuation.

Continuation proceeds by default. Not by authorization.
05

Responsibility Without Authority

Formal accountability assigned to an individual who lacks the institutional right to refuse continuation.

Named after. Not named before.
06

Continuation Bias

The institutional preference for execution over pause, even when named authorization has not been confirmed.

Default continuation displaces default refusal.
07

Refusal Penalty

Informal institutional cost imposed on those who invoke refusal authority in good faith.

When refusal carries a cost, it will not be invoked.
08

Authorization Oversight

The review mechanism governing use of refusal authority and authorization over refusal.

Prevents arbitrary veto. Prevents unchecked override.
