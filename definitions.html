Decision Authority Research
Definitions

Core Terms

These are not standard corporate terms.

They describe recurring governance failures that existing language often avoids naming clearly.

Independent conceptual research in accountability and stop authority.

01
Named Stop Authority

A single identified person formally empowered to stop a specific irreversible decision.

Named before execution begins.

Not a team. Not a role. A person.
02
Irreversible Decision

A decision that cannot be undone without disproportionate cost, harm, or loss.

The threshold should be recognized before it is crossed.

Late awareness is structurally useless.
03
Decision Authority Record (DAR)

A written record identifying who holds stop authority over a decision pathway.

Created before execution begins.

DAR names who can stop.
04
Bound Refusal

A formal obligation requiring intervention when defined risk conditions appear.

Authority includes duty, not only permission.

Observation alone is not governance.
05
Stop Authority Gap

No clearly named person can stop the decision when needed.

Momentum continues by default.

One of the most common hidden failure modes.
06
Escalation Window

A defined response period granted once a stop issue appears.

If unused, authority transfers automatically.

Timing is determined by operational risk.
07
Alternate Authority

A predefined successor who receives stop authority if the primary holder fails to act.

No critical control should depend on one unavailable person.

Redundancy prevents paralysis.
08
Oversight Trigger

Use of stop authority, refusal to act, delay, or transfer creates a mandatory review event.

The purpose is verification, governance integrity, and learning.

Every stop event should leave a documented trail.
09
Abuse Review

Every stop action may be reviewed for legitimacy, evidence basis, timing, and proportionality.

This protects the organization while preserving real stop power.

Review misuse. Do not remove authority.
10
Distributed Permission

Many people can approve continuation, yet no single person can stop.

Consensus often dissolves accountability.

Everyone approves. Nobody owns.
11
Hostile Hold

Misuse of stop authority for political, personal, or unrelated motives.

Unchecked obstruction can damage the organization as much as absent authority.

Bad use requires controls.
12
Cosmetic Governance

Processes that appear rigorous yet cannot interrupt dangerous momentum.

Documentation exists, but no real stop path exists.

Looks safe. Stops nothing.
13
Momentum Trap

A decision continues because it has already started, not because it remains justified.

Movement is mistaken for validity.

Past effort becomes false evidence.
14
Liability Drift

Responsibility shifts downward while decision power remains elsewhere.

Exposure moves faster than authority.

Common before visible failure.
If no one can clearly identify who may stop it, the gap already exists.

Most frameworks explain approval.

Far fewer define who must stop continuation when risk rises.

DAR begins there.
