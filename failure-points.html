Decision Authority Research — Failure Points

Where accountability breaks

Three patterns. One missing element.

These are normal operations.
What is missing is not data, policy, or review.

Before it breaks, it operates as “we”.
After it breaks, it becomes a name.
01 — Clinical Governance
The protocol updates. No one is named to stop it.

A hospital protocol change affects drug dosage for a specific patient population. Three people reviewed it. The update goes live automatically after committee approval. Two weeks later, adverse events are reported.

The gap: The committee approved. No individual was named to stop. No stop authority existed.
02 — Financial Services
The system continues. The next meeting is in four days.

A trading system detects conditions it was never designed for. It continues executing. The risk committee meets weekly. By the time they convene, the position is irreversible.

The gap: Escalation existed. Real-time stop authority did not. No one was named to act before the meeting.
03 — Agentic AI
The team approved the release. No one was named to halt it.

A model reaches 40,000 users before anyone reviews outputs. Behavior deviates. The system acts faster than governance can respond. By the time the escalation begins, the exposure is already at scale.

The gap: Everyone was responsible. No one was named. The team is not a stop authority.
In each case, the organization knew how to approve.
None had defined who could stop.
When a decision becomes irreversible under uncertainty,
who is named to stop it?

If the answer is unclear, distributed, or delayed — the exposure already exists.
