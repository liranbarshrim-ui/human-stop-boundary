Degraded Authority

Authority
Drift

Refusal authority can be clearly assigned and still disappear.
The person remains. The authority quietly does not.

The title stays.
The authority
does not travel with it.
The Deeper Problem

Assigned authority
and exercised authority
are not the same thing.

Authority drift does not require anyone to remove a person's authority. It only requires that exercising that authority becomes progressively less expected than allowing continuation.

The authority remains assigned. The expectation that it will be exercised does not.

A person may retain full authority on paper. But if exercising that authority carries personal cost — institutional, political, reputational — while allowing continuation carries none, the structure has already shifted.

The question is not only who holds refusal authority.
The question is whether the organization still expects
it to be exercised when it matters.
The Mechanism

Authority rarely disappears
through revocation.

No one removed the authority. No formal decision was made. The organizational chart was not updated. The title remained unchanged.

But the authority to refuse continuation eroded through something quieter: accumulated pressure, repeated escalations that went unresolved, and a slow organizational understanding that refusal would carry personal cost.

Authority drift is not absence.
It is the gap between the authority that appears on paper
and the authority that functions under pressure.
How It Happens

Drift does not require
a decision.
It requires silence.

Declared Named individual holds refusal authority.
Effective The same individual raises concerns. Continuation proceeds anyway. No resolution required.
Declared The role carries documented stop authority.
Effective Escalation replaces refusal. The concern is acknowledged. The process continues.
Declared The person is expected to interrupt continuation before irreversible conditions emerge.
Effective The person is no longer certain that refusal is theirs to exercise. Or that it will hold.

In each case, the authority still exists on paper. In each case, continuation is already happening without it.

The test is not whether
refusal is permitted.
The test is whether it stops anything.
The Distinction

A control point
and a refusal authority
are not the same thing.

A control point can slow continuation. It can generate records. It can flag concerns and require documentation. But continuation remains available — with or without resolution.

Refusal authority exists only when continuation cannot proceed without explicit release from the named individual.

Authority drift is what happens when a refusal authority
becomes a control point —
without anyone deciding that it should.
Authority that disappears under pressure
was a control point with a title.
What It Means

DAR does not recognize declared authority — only effective authority. The operative test is binary and must be answerable before the event: if the identified party says no, does continuation stop?

Authority drift is observable when the answer changes over time — not because the structure changed, but because the expectation did.

An organization may believe it has named refusal authority. The question is whether that authority still functions when it costs something to exercise it.

Once the question is asked, the absence can no longer remain invisible.

The person remains.
The authority quietly does not.
