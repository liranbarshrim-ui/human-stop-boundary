Authority
drift.

Refusal authority can be clearly assigned and still disappear. Not through a single decision. Through gradual erosion that leaves no visible trace.

The person still exists.
The authority no longer does.
The organizational chart did not change.
The appointment was never revoked.
The authority drifted anyway.
This is not a story about people who failed.

It is a story about structures that allowed authority to become technically present and operationally absent.
How authority drifts — without anyone deciding it should
01
The person changes. The title survives. The responsibility to refuse quietly does not.
02
Escalation becomes routine. What was designed as an exception becomes a workflow. Refusal stops being expected. Continuation becomes the operational default.
03
Continuation grows costly to interrupt. Schedules, commitments, and momentum accumulate. Refusal becomes socially expensive before it becomes structurally impossible.
04
The record still shows a named authority. Audits pass. Reviews conclude nothing is missing. Refusal authority is technically present and operationally absent.
Authority is not tested in calm conditions.
It is tested when continuation becomes costly.
The question is not whether someone is named.
The question is whether the named person
can still refuse — now, under current pressure,
against the people currently in the room.

Does the authority
survive the appointment?

Or does it drift quietly into the record?

What was assigned in advance, before pressure, before ambiguity, before organizational reinterpretation?

The structural question →
The appointment is the protection.

But only if the authority survives the appointment.
