Clarity supports judgment.
It does not replace it.

Scope, limits, and intended use. What this work is for. What it is not. And what remains the responsibility of people, not frameworks.

Purpose

What this work is for.

This work exists to identify a structural gap in governance: the absence of a named individual authorized to interrupt continuation before irreversible conditions emerge.

It is intended to support organizations in clarifying authority, reducing preventable exposure, and naming who can interrupt continuation before it becomes irreversible.

A framework can define accountability.
Only people can exercise it.

Clarity does not eliminate difficult decisions. It makes responsibility visible before pressure arrives.

Limits

What this work is not.

This work does not replace legal, regulatory, technical, or fiduciary responsibility. It does not certify compliance, guarantee outcomes, or eliminate risk. Responsibility for decisions remains with officers, boards, operators, and designated decision-makers.

Policies do not stop events. Committees do not stop events. Slides do not stop events. Timely authority exercised by a named person can.

Principles

Good faith means substance.

Authority is human.
Documents may assign power. Only identifiable people can exercise it when timing matters.
No cosmetic governance.
Processes that look serious but cannot interrupt danger are appearances, not control.
Stop power needs oversight.
Unchecked stop authority can become veto abuse. No stop authority can become unmanaged momentum.
Escalation must be real.
If authority is unclear during pressure, the framework has already failed.
Governance should be real.
Application should be proportionate, lawful, and directed toward genuine governance improvement — not the appearance of governance.
The Question

If the decision mattered tomorrow,
would authority be clear today?

Not in a policy document.
Not in an org chart.
In a name.
