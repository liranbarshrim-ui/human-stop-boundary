Governance Gap

Approval is not refusal.

Most governance systems explain how continuation is managed. Very few define who is authorized to refuse irreversible continuation before execution proceeds.

Observation is not authority.
Escalation is not interruption.
Approval is not refusal.

Existing Governance Layers

Modern organizations already contain extensive safeguards.

Human-In-The-Loop

Human validation before automated execution or escalation.

RACI Structures

Responsibility assignment across execution, approval, consultation, and visibility.

KPI Governance

Measurement systems designed to optimize operational performance and outcomes.

Risk Committees

Oversight structures responsible for evaluating organizational risk.

Compliance Controls

Procedural governance systems designed to maintain regulatory alignment.

Audit Systems

Post-event accountability, verification, and traceability mechanisms.

The Structural Gap

The missing layer is often refusal authority.

Most governance frameworks define: who reviewed, who approved, who monitored, who escalated, or who complied with process.

Very few define who can explicitly refuse irreversible continuation once institutional momentum already exists.

RACI defines responsibility.
DAR defines refusal authority.

Decision Authority Research

The DAR question is simple.

Before irreversible execution proceeds under uncertainty:

Who, by name, is authorized to refuse continuation?
