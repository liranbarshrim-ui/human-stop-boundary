Missing Authority

Approval is
not refusal.

Oversight does not necessarily create refusal authority.

Everyone reviewed it.
No one refused.
Observation is not authority.
Escalation alone does not create refusal authority.
Approval is not refusal.
A system can contain oversight, human review, escalation, and approval, and still leave the question of stopping unanswered.

That is not safety.
That is the appearance of safety.
A distinction often left implicit
Possibility of refusal Someone could stop it.
No one is required to.
No one is named.
No one carries refusal authority.
Refusal authority Someone is named in advance.
Expected to decide.
Authorized to stop.
Refusal is carried by name.
Being able to stop is not the same as being responsible to stop.

If refusal belongs to everyone, refusal belongs to no one.
The question is never whether the system can continue.
The question is whether anyone inside it
is authorized to refuse.

Who, by name,
is authorized to refuse?

Who carries refusal — by name?

What changes when a stopping point is defined in advance?

Continuation was defined.
Refusal was not.
