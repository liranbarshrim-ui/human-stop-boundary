Why internal attempts fail

Most organizations can describe the decision.
They fail when asked to name the person.

Diffusion of responsibility

“We’ll escalate” replaces ownership.

When no one is required to hold the decision, everyone assumes someone else will. Escalation becomes the answer. By the time it reaches someone with authority, the window has closed.

Political cost

No one wants their name attached before the outcome is known.

Naming a stop authority before the decision means accepting accountability before there is anything to be accountable for. Most people avoid this. Most organizations do not ask them to do otherwise.

Timing collapse

Escalation happens after it is already irreversible.

Internal processes move at the speed of consensus. Irreversible decisions move faster. By the time the right person is notified, the decision has already made itself.

Internal processes optimize for alignment.
Irreversible decisions require ownership.
If you can assign a single name before the decision — you do not need this.

If you cannot — that is exactly where this starts.

If you cannot name the person — it is already happening.
