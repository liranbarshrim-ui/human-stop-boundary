DAR — case studies

Real failures.
Same pattern.

Real failures rarely begin with missing data.
They begin with missing authority.

In each case below, the signals existed. The warnings existed. The process existed.

What did not exist was one named person obligated to stop continuation in time.

Approval is easy.
Naming refusal is rare.

Different industries. Same missing authority.

01 Financial / Trading

Knight Capital Group

45 minutes  ·  $440 million lost  ·  2012

A software deployment activated an obsolete trading algorithm. Within 45 minutes the firm had accumulated a position it could not absorb.

The system was visible. The loss was accumulating in real time. No single individual carried the explicit authority and obligation to halt execution immediately.

By the time intervention arrived, the loss was already compounding.

The stop authority question
Who was named in advance to suspend deployment the moment execution diverged from intent?
02 Aviation

Boeing 737 MAX

346 fatalities  ·  Two crashes  ·  2018–2019

Warnings about the MCAS system existed internally. Escalation channels existed. Documentation existed.

Continuation of certification and delivery proceeded across multiple organizational layers. No clearly identified individual appears to have held final stop authority when the risk signal crossed a threshold.

Responsibility, when it resolved, was distributed. The harm was not.

The stop authority question
Who held explicit, named authority to halt certification before the second aircraft flew?
03 Banking / Conduct

Wells Fargo Account Fraud

3.5 million unauthorized accounts  ·  $3B+ in penalties  ·  2016

Unauthorized account creation continued for years across thousands of branch employees. Internal reports flagged the pattern. Compliance structures existed.

Continuation required no named individual to actively authorize it. It required only that no named individual was obligated to stop it.

The structure dispersed blame faster than it assigned responsibility.

The stop authority question
Who was named, in advance, with the authority and obligation to halt the sales program when the pattern became visible?
04 Technology / Infrastructure

CrowdStrike Global Outage

8.5 million devices  ·  Global operational disruption  ·  July 2024

A single content configuration update was deployed globally without staged rollout controls. The update caused immediate system failure across critical infrastructure sectors worldwide.

The deployment pipeline had technical gates. No single named individual is publicly identified as having the obligation to stop the deployment before global execution.

Speed of deployment outran the speed of human judgment.

The stop authority question
Who was personally obligated to authorize global deployment before it executed?
05 Financial / Markets

Flash Crash

Nearly $1 trillion briefly erased in market value  ·  May 6, 2010

Algorithmic trading cascades erased nearly a trillion dollars of market value in under fifteen minutes. Human oversight existed at every participating firm.

The speed gap was technical.
The inability to interrupt it was human.

No named individual at any single point in the chain carried the obligation to stop continuation.

The stop authority question
At what point was a human being named in advance to interrupt the cascade before it became self-reinforcing?
If you had to stop one decision today — by name — which one would it be?

These are not isolated incidents.

They share one recurring absence: no individual was named in advance to carry the obligation to refuse.

If you cannot name that person today, the pattern already exists.

And when it fails, that name will still be required.
