Decision Authority Research

Real Failures.
Same Pattern.

Across industries, catastrophic outcomes rarely begin with missing information.

They begin when warning signals exist, but no one is clearly empowered and obligated to stop continuation in time.

Approval is common. Named refusal is rare.
01 Financial Markets

Knight Capital

45 minutes • $440 million lost • 2012

A deployment failure triggered uncontrolled trading activity at machine speed.

The system was live. Losses were visible. Intervention lagged behind execution.

The stop authority question

Who was explicitly empowered to halt execution the moment behavior diverged from intent?

02 Aviation

Boeing 737 MAX

346 fatalities • 2018–2019

Warnings, concerns, and escalation signals existed across multiple layers.

Yet continuation persisted until tragedy forced a response.

The stop authority question

Who had clear authority to stop continuation before the second crash occurred?

03 Banking Conduct

Wells Fargo

Millions of unauthorized accounts • Multi-billion penalties

Problematic incentives continued for years while signals accumulated internally.

Stopping required action from someone specific. That clarity was absent.

The stop authority question

Who was obligated to stop the program once the pattern became visible?

04 Infrastructure

CrowdStrike Global Outage

Millions of affected devices • 2024

A global update propagated faster than human recovery capacity.

Technical pipelines moved immediately. Human interruption came later.

The stop authority question

Who personally owned the go / no-go decision before worldwide release?

05 Markets

Flash Crash

Massive temporary market value destruction • 2010

Automated cascades outran human oversight.

Speed became a governance risk.

The stop authority question

Who was named in advance to interrupt the cascade before it reinforced itself?

If one irreversible decision in your organization had to be stopped today, by name, who would that be?
