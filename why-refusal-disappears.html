The Structural Explanation

Why Refusal
Disappears

Refusal authority does not require revocation.
It only requires structures that make continuation
easier than stopping.

Continuation rarely needs a defender.
Refusal always does.
Not Intent. Structure.

Organizations do not eliminate
refusal authority.
They outweigh it.

The disappearance of refusal authority does not require a decision to remove it. It requires incentives that favor continuation over stopping.

When continuation is rewarded and refusal carries cost, the structure has already answered the question — before the moment of uncertainty arrives.

No one eliminates the authority. The conditions simply make it progressively less likely to be exercised.

Refusal disappears when continuation
becomes easier to justify than stopping.
The Asymmetry

Continuation and refusal
do not carry
equal weight.

Continuation Distributes accountability across teams, functions, and time.
Refusal Concentrates accountability in a named individual at a specific moment.
Continuation Produces visible short-term results. Progress is measurable.
Refusal Produces no visible result. The value of stopping is the harm that did not occur.
Continuation Requires no justification beyond the existing approval.
Refusal Requires justification under conditions of uncertainty, where certainty is unavailable by definition.
Continuation Failure, if it occurs, is shared. Responsibility is distributed.
Refusal If the refusal was wrong, it is personal. The person who stopped carries it.

Given this asymmetry, refusal does not disappear because people lack courage. It disappears because the structure did not account for the cost of exercising it.

The value of stopping
is the harm that did not occur.
Invisible. Unmeasured. Unrewarded.
What This Means

Named authority without
structural support
is not stop authority.

Assigning refusal authority to a named individual is necessary. It is not sufficient.

If the person named to stop carries personal cost for stopping while continuation carries none, the assignment is nominal. The authority exists on paper. The conditions make it progressively harder to use.

This is why DAR examines not only who is named, but whether the named individual can exercise that authority without the organization quietly making continuation the path of least resistance.

A name without structural support
is a title, not stop authority.
The Question

When an organization names someone to stop, it must also ask what happens to that person when they do.

Not whether they are permitted to stop.
Whether stopping, under the actual conditions they will face, is structurally supported or structurally penalized.

If the answer is unclear before the moment arrives, the authority may exist on paper and disappear precisely when it is needed.

Refusal disappears
not because
nobody cared.
Because stopping
cost more
than continuing.
