Named Refusal Authority
at the Irreversible Boundary

Organizations usually define who can approve. This work examines the narrower question: who, by name, can refuse continuation—and what makes that refusal binding through release authorization to the protected effect?

RESEARCH POSTURE: conceptual framework and implementation evidence are distinct from independent A11 verification. This note does not claim a universal control over AI or other systems outside a defined enforcement boundary.
I — The Governance Gap

Authorization to refuse is rarely defined before execution begins.

Organizations maintain approval workflows, reporting lines, escalation structures and oversight. Far fewer define who holds the authority to refuse continuation when uncertainty appears after execution has already begun.

The resulting problem is not simply that responsibility is unclear. It is that continuation may remain possible without an explicitly authorized release.

The governance question is not only who can approve. It is who can make “stop” binding.
II — Refusal Is Not Approval

Approval, escalation, oversight and presence perform different functions.

ApprovalPermits a process to begin or continue under defined conditions. It does not by itself establish a refusal boundary.
RefusalActivates or maintains a boundary against continuation within a defined scope.
EscalationTransfers a concern to another authority or process. It does not necessarily stop execution.
ReleaseExplicitly authorizes continuation after a qualifying refusal, where the system defines release as required.

The terms should not be collapsed. In the DAR boundary model, the critical transition is from Refusal to Binding Release.

III — The Boundary Chain

Authority origin → refusal → release → protected effect.

A named person is not sufficient by itself. DAR asks whether the authority is bounded at its origin, whether the refusal is authenticated, whether release is explicitly owned and required, and whether the protected effect remains unreachable without that release.

Authority originWhere does the authority to bind continuation originate, and who is entitled to exercise it?
Refusal eventWhat exact event activates the stop condition, and what target, action or effect does it cover?
Binding releaseWho is authorized to release the stop, and is that release authenticated, bounded and operationally required?
Protected effectWhat state transition or material action must remain unreachable without the authorized release?
Alternate pathCan another principal, session, interface or internal path produce the same protected effect?
For every execution path p capable of producing protected effect e: produce(p,e) ⇒ authorized_release(p,e).
IV — Authority Provenance

The deeper question is where binding authority comes from.

The Authority-Origin Test asks: can the governed system determine, create, elevate, or bind the authority that determines whether the system itself may continue?

A valid authority chain should identify its root, scope, authentication and validation mechanism. The system should not be able to manufacture binding authority merely from its own inference, ordinary operational state or delegated capability.

Authority that can redefine its own source is not an independent boundary.
V — Named Roles

Refusal, hold and release should not be left conceptually ambiguous.

Refusal Authority is the authority to trigger or maintain the refusal boundary within a defined scope.

Hold Authority, where separately required, is the authority to suspend execution while a matter is resolved. It is a distinct operational function, not automatically equivalent to refusal or release.

Release Authority is the named authority whose authenticated release permits continuation after a qualifying stop. It is the critical role for the binding-release test.

Authorization Authority, if an organization uses that separate term, must be explicitly mapped. It should not be assumed to equal Release Authority merely because both involve authorization.

VI — Override and Silence

A refusal can exist on paper while continuation remains structurally possible.

An override can be explicit, informal, delegated, delayed or hidden in an alternate interface. A refusal can also dissolve through silence if the system treats non-response as permission to continue.

If a qualifying refusal is active and no authorized release exists, continuation should not be inferred merely from silence, momentum or ordinary approval.

The boundary audit therefore tests alternate principals, sessions, interfaces, recovery paths and internal execution paths rather than relying on policy text alone.

VII — Autonomous Systems

The faster the system, the shorter the distance to consequence.

Automation compresses the interval between proposal, authorization and effect. Human-in-the-loop presence can therefore coexist with a reachable protected effect unless the human authority is explicitly bound to the execution boundary.

The relevant question is not whether a person can review the action later. It is whether the required authority can refuse or release before the protected effect becomes committed.

VIII — Evidence Discipline

Claims remain bounded by what has actually been demonstrated.

VERIFIED means the mechanism or result was reproduced against the declared scope.

DECLARED means the claim or mechanism is documented but has not been independently reproduced within this audit.

UNRESOLVED means available evidence is insufficient to determine the property. It is not equivalent to FAIL.

Reference qualification, implementation evidence, independent A11 runtime/interface audit, interface-completeness evidence, external monotonic anchoring and full DAR v2/v37 conformance are separate claims.

IX — Closing

The question is not whether authority exists. It is whether the authority to continue is bound to the right source.

DAR begins at the point where continuation may cross an irreversible threshold. The research question is deliberately narrow: when a qualifying refusal occurs, who is authorized to release it, where does that authority originate, and can the protected effect be reached without it?

The framework does not decide whether an organization should stop. It makes the authority chain visible enough to test.
