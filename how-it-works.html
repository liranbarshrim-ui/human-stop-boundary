Operational Structure

How refusal authority operates before irreversible execution continues.

DAR does not optimize systems. DAR interrupts continuation when irreversible execution crosses uncertainty thresholds.

The objective is not retrospective accountability. The objective is interruption before continuation becomes irreversible.

Continuation is not automatic.
Continuation must survive explicit authorization.
Execution Structure

Most systems are designed to continue.

Approval, deployment, escalation, monitoring, and optimization all reinforce continuity.

DAR introduces an interruption layer before irreversible execution proceeds under uncertainty.

Threshold Logic

The interruption point matters more than the outcome.

DAR operates before final failure.

The relevant condition is the transition from reversible execution into irreversible exposure under uncertainty.

The issue is not whether the system failed. The issue is whether continuation required named authorization before the irreversible threshold was crossed.

If nobody is required to authorize continuation,
continuation becomes procedural momentum.
Protected Refusal

Refusal authority must remain institutionally survivable.

A stop mechanism without protection collapses under operational pressure.

Where refusal creates political exposure, financial punishment, or career risk, continuation becomes structurally favored.

DAR assumes that stopping carries cost. That is precisely why refusal authority must exist before continuation proceeds.

Systems scale execution.
DAR scales interruption responsibility.
