· Decision Authority Research · Research Note · Working model v0.2 · May 2026

This is an observational research note, not a governance framework.
It does not claim to explain organizational failure.
It asks a narrower question: where does refusal authority survive when the person disappears?

The question
Layer I
Who, by name, is authorized to refuse continuation before a decision becomes irreversible?
Layer II
If that person disappeared tomorrow — where would refusal authority live?
Where authority can live
  • Person Lives in an individual. Not transferable. Disappears with them.
  • Role Attached to a title. Survives only if explicitly bounded and documented as belonging to the role, not the individual. Vulnerable to regression.
  • Structure (Held) Encoded in governance artifacts. Has demonstrably survived pressure. No succession decision required.
  • Structure (Declared) Written down but untested under pressure. Indistinguishable from Role without a stress event.
  • Unlocated No recoverable authority anchor could be identified. Distinct from Role: not absent, unlocatable.
  • Unknown Insufficient public evidence to classify.

What this note does not claim
Explicit boundaries
— That formal structure always governs. Structure (Declared) may not hold under pressure.
— That Role is stable. Role is a transitional state, always vulnerable to regression toward Person or Unlocated.
— That distributed systems resist classification entirely. They resist classification only when refusal authority is not explicitly separated from other authority.
— That this taxonomy is complete. Several cases remain Unknown. ICU protocols vary by institution.
— That governance failure follows from absence of named authority. Absence creates vulnerability. It does not determine outcome.
The reports named a person.
But a name is not a mechanism.
When the person disappeared, continuity remained undocumented.
 
A named authority is not yet an institutional authority.
The appointment is the beginning of the answer.
Continuity is the rest of it.
