# DAR Layer II: Authority Continuity

*One-page formulation — May 2026*

---

DAR asks a simple governance question:
Who, by name, is authorized to refuse continuation before a decision becomes irreversible?

That question assumes a name exists.

This asks what happens to that authority when the person holding it suddenly disappears.

---

**The reports named a person.**
**But a name is not a mechanism.**

A role can be refilled. A title can be reassigned. What cannot be automatically transferred is the authority itself — unless it was documented as belonging to the structure, not the person.

When the person disappears, the question becomes visible:
Did the authority live in them — or in the record?

---

There are three possible answers.

**In the person.** Not written anywhere. Not transferable. Disappears with them.

**In the role.** Attached to the title, not the individual. Survives — but only if the authority was actually scoped and documented as belonging to the role.

**In the documented structure.** Named, bounded, with an explicit continuity mechanism. This is what survives. This is what DAR requires.

The test is simple:

*If the named person were gone tomorrow — would a successor inherit the authority, or only the title?*

---

This is not a failure case. No crisis is required for the gap to exist.

The gap is visible only in documentation. It becomes legible only when something else goes wrong at the same moment.

DAR does not prevent death.
It prevents the situation where a person's death is also the death of the authority they carried.

---

A named authority is not yet an institutional authority.
The appointment is the beginning of the answer.
Continuity is the rest of it.
