Decision Authority Research

AI Governance for Irreversible Decisions

When systems move faster than governance can respond, control becomes theoretical.

Core Question
Who, by name, can stop the system before harm becomes irreversible?
The Governance Gap

Many organizations built approval processes, review committees, risk forums, and escalation paths.

Few built real-time stop authority.

That gap becomes critical when autonomous or semi-autonomous systems act faster than institutions decide.

What Changes With AI
SpeedModels can deploy, update, optimize, and scale faster than committees can react.
OpacityOutputs may be visible before causes are understood.
Distributed OwnershipMany teams participate. No single person owns the stop decision.
Escalation DelayBy the time escalation begins, exposure may already exist.
Approval is not control.
Control begins where someone can stop.
Questions Boards Should Ask

Who can suspend deployment immediately?

Who owns the gap between system speed and oversight speed?

Is human override operational or symbolic?

If harm starts now, who acts in 30 seconds?

Would that person be protected for stopping early?

Regulatory Direction

Global regulation increasingly focuses on human oversight, accountability, governance controls, and traceable responsibility.

The trend is clear: when automated systems create material outcomes, institutions must still identify accountable humans.

If no one is named,
the system is governing itself.
Practical Principle

When a decision becomes irreversible under uncertainty, someone must have authority to stop it — by name.
