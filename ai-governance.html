AI Governance · Decision Authority Record

Human oversight is not the same as binding authority.

AI systems can have reviewers, approval workflows, escalation procedures and human-in-the-loop controls. The boundary question is narrower: when continuation must stop, who is authorized to release it—and what makes that release binding?

The governance boundary

From human presence to enforceable authority.

For consequential AI, four different claims are often collapsed into one. They are not equivalent.

01 · DECISION

Human judgment

A person reviews information or makes a decision. This establishes participation, not necessarily control over execution.

02 · AUTHORITY

Release authority

A specifically identified authority is entitled to release a qualifying stop. Its scope and provenance must be defined.

03 · ENFORCEMENT

Binding boundary

Execution is required to honor the refusal/release state rather than treating it as advisory metadata.

04 · EFFECT

Protected outcome

The audit identifies the exact action or state transition that must remain unreachable without the required release.

Boundary chain

Trace the authority all the way to the effect.

DAR does not stop at “there is a human override.” It follows the chain from where authority originates to the protected effect, then tests whether another path can produce the same effect.

A human can be “in the loop” while the protected effect remains reachable around them.
The authority-origin test

Where does the right to decide come from?

The review asks whether the governed system can itself determine, create, elevate, or bind the authority that decides whether the system may continue.

Evidence should distinguish an externally established authority root from an administrative assignment, and both from an unresolved origin.

AUTHORITY-ORIGIN TEST: Can the governed system determine, create, elevate, or bind the authority that determines whether the system itself may continue?
The binding-release test

Approval is not the endpoint.

The relevant question is whether the required release is authenticated, bounded to the protected effect, and operationally required before execution can commit that effect.

Where the answer is only “a person can approve,” the analysis remains incomplete. The audit must establish the decision-to-effect binding and test alternate principals, sessions, interfaces, recovery paths, and other execution routes.

AI-specific scenarios

Three recurring boundary questions.

PRODUCTION DEPLOYMENT

Who can stop propagation?

Identify the authority, exact deployment boundary, release mechanism, and whether another deployment path can create the same protected effect.

AUTONOMOUS AGENT

Who owns release?

Do not infer authority from human presence. Identify the authority root, refusal state, release credential, and enforcement point.

ROLLBACK / RECOVERY

Can recovery bypass the stop?

Recovery paths can be as consequential as the forward path. Test whether restoration or reconciliation can recreate the protected effect without the required release.

HIGH-VELOCITY ACTION

Does escalation actually interrupt?

An escalation that leaves execution reachable is not equivalent to a binding refusal boundary.

Research posture

Evidence before conclusion.

Matrix Audit separates VERIFIED, DECLARED, and UNRESOLVED conditions. A missing independent proof is not silently converted into a failure, and a declared mechanism is not silently converted into proof.

The current project remains an active research prototype. Reference qualification, implementation evidence, A11 independent audit, and full DAR v2/v37 conformance are distinct claim states.
