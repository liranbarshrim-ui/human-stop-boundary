Decision Authority Research
Methodology

Refusal authority must exist before irreversible execution becomes operational.

The DAR methodology examines how continuation becomes institutionally automatic under uncertainty.

The objective is not retrospective blame. The objective is interruption legitimacy before irreversible continuation proceeds.

The methodology does not ask whether a system can execute.

It asks whether continuation is legitimately authorized.
Method Structure
Step 01

Identify irreversible execution conditions.

Determine where operational continuation produces outcomes that cannot be meaningfully reversed once execution proceeds.

The analysis focuses on execution environments where rollback, correction, or institutional recovery becomes structurally limited after continuation.

Step 02

Map continuity pressure.

Examine where institutional systems favor continuation through operational momentum, procedural legitimacy, distributed responsibility, financial incentives, or political pressure.

Continuation often becomes easier than interruption long before anyone explicitly decides to continue.

Step 03

Locate refusal authority.

Identify whether interruption authority exists by name before continuation proceeds.

The analysis distinguishes between theoretical authority and operationally usable authority under real institutional conditions.

Step 04

Evaluate interruption survivability.

Determine whether refusal can occur without unacceptable institutional, political, operational, or career-level punishment.

Where interruption creates disproportionate exposure for the individual required to stop execution, continuation becomes structurally favored.

Step 05

Measure legitimacy conditions.

Examine whether continuation remains institutionally legitimate under uncertainty when interruption authority is absent, delayed, fragmented, or structurally unusable.

The methodology evaluates legitimacy independently from technical success, validation status, or procedural completion.

A system may remain operationally functional
while institutionally unauthorized.
Core Principle

Continuation is not neutral once interruption becomes structurally unavailable.

Where irreversible execution exists, the absence of named refusal authority transforms continuation into an unexamined institutional default.

The methodology therefore evaluates not only execution capability, but interruption legitimacy before execution proceeds.

DAR focuses on the structural conditions that allow continuation to proceed automatically under uncertainty.

Method Boundary

DAR is not a monitoring layer, compliance mechanism, or anomaly detection system.

The methodology does not optimize operational performance.

It examines whether irreversible continuation remains legitimate once interruption becomes institutionally difficult, politically costly, or psychologically unavailable.

The focus is governance legitimacy before irreversible execution — not failure analysis after the fact.
