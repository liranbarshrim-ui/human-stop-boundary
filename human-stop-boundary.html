Concept Definition

Human Stop Boundary

Definition & Origin  ·  2026

A Human Stop Boundary is the point at which a decision becomes irreversible under uncertainty, requiring a specifically named individual with explicit authority to stop it.

Origin

The term Human Stop Boundary was introduced by Liran Bar-Shrim in 2026 as part of Decision Authority Research (DAR). It emerged from a recurring observation across regulated industries: the gap between those who hold information and those with named authority to stop is not a structural accident. It is a design choice.

Why It Exists
  • Processes diffuse responsibility across roles, functions, and committees.
  • No single name is committed in advance to stop before execution becomes irreversible.
  • When the moment arrives, authority is unclear and failure follows before anyone acts.
  • The larger the institution, the more bureaucratic the process — and the more invisible the boundary.
What This Is Not

This is not a policy or compliance framework. It is a question of authority. Who, by name, can stop the system before execution becomes irreversible?
