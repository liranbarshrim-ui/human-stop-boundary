Questions Asked When Responsibility Is Real

Common questions from boards, founders, operators, counsel, and leaders facing consequential decisions already in motion.

If no one can answer now,
the authority gap is already active.
01
What is DAR?
A written record naming one person with explicit authority to stop a specific irreversible decision before execution.
02
Who usually needs this?
Boards, executives, founders, regulated operators, AI teams, and firms where many approve but no one owns the final stop decision.
03
Does this apply to AI systems?
Yes. If systems move faster than governance responds, human control may become symbolic.
04
What counts as irreversible?
Any decision whose consequences cannot be undone quickly enough to avoid material harm.
05
Approval authority vs stop authority?
Approval allows continuation. Stop authority prevents continuation until explicit release.
06
What if the board already approved it?
Conditions may change after approval. Execution risk can still require active stop authority.
07
Who is exposed if failure happens?
Often the visible signatory, executive owner, director, or function leader.
08
Is this legal advice?
No. This is independent advisory work. Legal matters belong with qualified counsel.
09
What if nobody is named?
Then accountability often becomes unclear until after damage occurs.
10
Can authority be delegated?
Yes, if delegation is explicit, documented, and operationally real.
11
Who can override stop authority?
Only a clearly defined superior authority under known conditions.
12
Is this only for large firms?
No. Smaller companies often need clarity even more because roles overlap.
13
Can this help after failure?
It can help reconstruct responsibility gaps and improve future governance.
14
What sectors need this most?
Finance, AI, healthcare, cyber, critical infrastructure, public companies, and high velocity startups.
