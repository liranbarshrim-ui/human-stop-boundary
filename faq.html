Frequently Asked Questions

Questions about continuation, refusal authority, and irreversible execution.

Decision Authority Research examines the human and structural conditions required before irreversible continuation proceeds.

What is Decision Authority Research?
Decision Authority Research examines irreversible execution, refusal authority, institutional momentum, and named authorization before continuation proceeds.
What is the core question behind DAR?
The central question is simple: who, by name, has the authority to stop continuation before irreversible execution proceeds?
Why does continuation become dangerous?
In many organizations, continuation carries less personal cost than interruption. Over time, systems optimize for continuation automatically — even when uncertainty remains unresolved.
What is refusal authority?
Refusal authority is the explicit operational ability to interrupt or refuse continuation before irreversible execution occurs.
What does “named authorization” mean?
Named authorization means a specific individual is explicitly responsible for authorizing continuation. Without named responsibility, continuation becomes structurally automatic.
Is DAR a legal framework?
No. DAR is a governance and decision-structure research framework focused on authority, interruption, accountability, and continuation under uncertainty.
Does DAR oppose automation or AI?
No. DAR examines the governance conditions required before irreversible automated execution proceeds. The focus is not technology itself — but human authority before continuation.
Why is interruption important?
A system that can continue but cannot meaningfully stop itself becomes structurally unstable under uncertainty.
What problem does DAR identify?
Organizations often distribute responsibility across systems, teams, committees, and workflows. As responsibility diffuses, refusal disappears.
What happens when nobody is required to refuse continuation?
Continuation becomes automatic. Not because everyone agreed — but because interruption no longer exists operationally inside the structure.
