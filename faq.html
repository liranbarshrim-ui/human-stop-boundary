Decision Authority Research — FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

On stop authority, irreversible decisions, and AI governance.

01
What is DAR — Decision Authority Record?

DAR is a written record that names one individual with explicit authority to stop a specific irreversible decision before it executes. It is not a policy, framework, or software tool. It is a pre-commitment: one person, one decision pathway, defined in advance.

02
Who needs a Decision Authority Record?

Any organization where irreversible decisions can be made without a single named person having authority to stop them. This includes boards, CROs, General Counsel, AI governance teams, regulated financial operators, and executives carrying personal liability for high-stakes decisions.

03
Does DAR apply to AI systems?

Yes. Agentic AI systems can act, plan, and execute at machine speed. If no named individual has real-time authority to halt a model before its actions become irreversible, no stop authority exists. DAR maps who holds that authority before deployment — not after harm.

04
What counts as an irreversible decision?

A decision is irreversible when its consequences cannot be undone within a timeframe that prevents harm. Examples: an AI model deployed to production at scale, a trading position that exceeds threshold, a clinical protocol that goes live before adverse events are detected, a regulatory filing once submitted.

05
What is the difference between approval authority and stop authority?

Approval authority allows a decision to proceed. Stop authority makes continuation impossible without explicit release. Most organizations have approval structures. Very few define who can make continuation impossible. If continuation is available without a named release, no stop exists.

06
How is DAR different from standard governance or risk committees?

Committees approve. DAR names one person who can stop. A committee can review, recommend, and document. It cannot make continuation impossible. DAR does not replace governance structures. It answers the question they consistently leave open: who, by name, stops this before it becomes irreversible?

07
Who should have authority to stop an AI system?

One named individual with the technical ability, organizational authority, and pre-committed obligation to halt the system before its actions become irreversible. Not a team. Not an escalation path. A person defined before deployment, not identified after harm. This is what DAR establishes.

08
Why can’t organizations define stop authority internally?

Most organizations can explain their decision process. They fail when asked to name one person who can stop a critical decision in real time. Naming one person creates visible personal exposure — and most internal governance structures are designed, intentionally or not, to avoid that clarity.

09
How long does an engagement take?

A Decision Boundary Review (single pathway) is delivered in 5 to 7 business days. A Decision Accountability Review (multiple pathways) is delivered in 10 to 14 business days. Board Exposure Reviews are scoped individually.

10
