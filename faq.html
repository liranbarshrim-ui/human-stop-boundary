Liran Bar-Shrim Correspondence

Questions about
interruption authority.

On continuation, named authorization, and the structural conditions required before irreversible execution proceeds.

What is this research about?
It examines a structural question in governance:
Not who reviewed it. Not who approved it. Who could stop it.
What is the central question?
Before irreversible execution proceeds — who, by name, can still say no?
What is refusal authority?
The explicit operational ability to interrupt or refuse continuation before irreversible execution occurs. Not the ability to object. Not the ability to escalate. The authority to stop.
Why does continuation become automatic?
In many organizations, interruption carries higher operational and personal friction than continuation. Over time, systems can optimize for continuation — even when uncertainty remains unresolved. Interruption requires named authority. Continuation often does not.
What does "named authorization" mean?
A specific individual is explicitly identified in advance to interrupt continuation before irreversible conditions emerge.

Not a committee.
Not a general function.
Someone identifiable before the moment arrives.
What happens in real time?
Formal authority may exist on paper.
Under pressure, continuation can shift toward whoever is operationally available at the moment.

The question is whether interruption authority remains clear when continuation matters.
What if interruption authority exists only in procedure?
If interruption authority requires reopening policy documents during time pressure, continuation may already be operationally underway.

The question is not whether authority exists.
The question is whether it remains practically available when time matters.
Is this a legal framework?
No. This is independent writing and research focused on governance structure, authority, and the conditions under which continuation becomes irreversible. It is not legal advice and does not constitute legal guidance.
Does this oppose automation or AI?
No. The focus is not technology. The focus is the human authorization structure required before irreversible automated execution proceeds.
What happens when nobody is required to refuse continuation?
Continuation becomes automatic. Not because everyone agreed — but because interruption becomes practically unavailable inside the structure.
What problem does distributed responsibility create?
As responsibility spreads across systems, teams, and committees, interruption authority does not spread with it. It often becomes structurally unclear.
Where does this question matter?
Anywhere irreversible execution can proceed without clearly designated interruption authority.

The question is not only whether interruption authority exists, but whether it remains practically available when continuation matters most. The structural conditions that make that possible remain under active research.
