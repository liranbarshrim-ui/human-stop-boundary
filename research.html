The question beneath governance.

Before a consequential decision becomes irreversible, who, by name, is authorized to refuse continuation?

A control point can influence a decision. A refusal authority can prevent it.
01 / Theory

DAR

Decision Authority Record examines whether refusal authority is named, bounded, and actually binding within the defined enforcement boundary.

 02 / Failure mode

Authority drift

Responsibility can remain visible while practical authority quietly disappears through escalation, override, delegation, or momentum.

 03 / Evidence

Governance observations

Cases and reconstructions across safety, software, finance, manufacturing, clinical systems, and AI governance.

Research in progress — DAR-MA

DAR-MA explores outcome-level refusal authority in distributed, multi-agent systems. It is an experimental research branch, not part of the validated v36.14 claim set.

01 / Model-checkable

Declared transition system

For a finite, explicitly defined transition system, a Human Stop Cut can be checked against the declared graph: every path to the protected outcome must cross an enforcement cut.

02 / Assumption-bounded

Explicit trust boundary

Claims depend on what the system can define, observe, attribute, and enforce. Hidden interfaces, compromised infrastructure, or missing model coverage remain outside the established claim.

03 / Open-world

No universal theorem

For arbitrary programs, agents, tools, and external-world behavior, DAR does not assume that reachability can be completely decided or that every path can be modeled.

A Human Stop Cut is a property of a declared transition system, not a general theorem about arbitrary programs, agents, tools, or the external world.

In unrestricted systems, reachability may be undecidable; DAR therefore treats model completeness and boundary fidelity as explicit assumptions and evidence targets rather than silently assuming them.

We are not asking you to confirm DAR.
We are asking you to break it.

A successful counterexample is more valuable than an endorsement. The research challenge is to identify a concrete bypass, contradiction, or missing assumption within the declared claim.

This work is deliberately narrower than a general governance score. It does not decide whether an organization should stop. It asks whether the organization has intentionally defined who can stop, when they can stop, and whether continuation can proceed despite that refusal.
