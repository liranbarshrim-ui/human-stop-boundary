Private Governance Advisory

Authority must exist before exposure begins.

Most organizations define how decisions move forward. Very few define who can stop them when uncertainty rises and consequences become irreversible.

Request Private Access
The Problem

Approval systems scale. Personal accountability does not.

When something fails, institutions often search for who approved it, who signed it, or who owned the process. But in many critical moments, the deeper failure happened earlier: no one was clearly empowered to stop the decision in time.

Decision Authority Record

One decision. One threshold. One named authority.

DAR is a governance framework that identifies a consequential decision pathway, defines the moment continuation should pause, and names one accountable person with explicit authority to stop it before damage becomes irreversible.

Boards

Where collective approval can hide personal ownership until after failure.

Executives

Where speed, pressure and ambiguity outpace clear authority lines.

Legal & Risk

Where signatures exist, but real intervention power is uncertain.

AI Governance

Where systems can execute faster than oversight can react.

“If no one can be named to stop it, it may already be misgoverned.”
Why It Matters

The most exposed person is often not the one who caused the failure.

It is often the person who accepted responsibility without first ensuring authority existed. DAR addresses that gap before blame, regulators, investors or headlines do.
