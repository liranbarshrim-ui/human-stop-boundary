Decision Authority Research

When a decision becomes irreversible under uncertainty,
someone must have the authority to stop it.

When momentum would decide, DAR gives leadership one more decision before consequences harden.

If no one is named to stop it, it should not run. Private Inquiry
The Gap

Most organizations know how to approve decisions.
Almost none know who can stop them.

Approval workflows, escalation structures, budget authority. These are well-defined in most organizations.

They built systems where failure belongs to everyone,
so it belongs to no one.

Who can interrupt momentum immediately when new risk appears? Where this is unclear, responsibility becomes personal after the fact.

What DAR Changes

Without DAR, momentum decides. With DAR, a named authority creates one final moment for review, pause, or reversal.

When momentum would decide, DAR gives leadership one more decision.

The Framework
01

Identify Exposure

Locate decisions where execution may outrun governance and become irreversible.

02

Name Authority

Define one person, by name, who carries the explicit right and obligation to stop before execution.

03

Define Threshold

Specify in advance what conditions trigger the stop obligation.

04

Create Record

Document who stops, who releases, and what the default state is when no one acts.

Stop is the default state.
Continuation does not exist
until a named individual releases it.
