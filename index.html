Liran Bar-Shrim Correspondence

Approval is
not refusal.

A system can contain oversight, human review, escalation, and approval —

and still contain nobody authorized to stop irreversible continuation.

Everyone reviewed it.
Nobody stopped it.

No one refused.
So continuation
became automatic.

The structure contains oversight.
It does not contain refusal authority.

AI systems, automated execution, and distributed approval chains increasingly separate the moment of continuation from any named individual authorized to refuse it.

Observation is not authority.
Escalation is not interruption.
Approval is not refusal.

Most governance structures define who approves.
Almost none define who can refuse.

As responsibility distributes across functions and systems, refusal authority does not distribute with it. It disappears.

The question is never whether the system can continue.
The question is whether anyone inside it
is authorized to refuse.

One person. One structural question.

No institution behind it.
Just the question itself.

Who, by name,
is authorized to refuse?
