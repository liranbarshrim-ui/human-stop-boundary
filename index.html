The Human Moment

No irreversible execution without named authorization.

Every irreversible system eventually reaches the same human moment: someone can continue, but nobody is required to stop continuation.

At that point, continuation becomes automatic. Not because everyone agreed — but because refusal no longer exists inside the structure.

Organizations rarely collapse because nobody approved the system.

They collapse because nobody was required to refuse continuation.

Structural Reality

The system continues by default.

In most organizations, continuation carries less personal cost than interruption. The result is predictable: execution proceeds even when uncertainty remains unresolved.

Meetings occur. Reviews happen. Dashboards remain green. But the authority to stop irreversible continuation never becomes operational.

Distributed Responsibility

When responsibility is spread across systems, committees, and workflows, refusal disappears into process.

Institutional Momentum

Once execution begins, continuation becomes structurally easier than interruption.

Human Cost

If stopping carries political, financial, or organizational cost, most systems silently optimize for continuation.

Named Authority

A stop mechanism only exists if a specific individual is explicitly authorized to refuse continuation.

Decision Authority Research

DAR examines the point before continuation.

Decision Authority Research studies refusal authority, irreversible execution, institutional momentum, and governance legitimacy before continuation proceeds.

The central question is not whether a system can continue. The central question is whether anyone is required — by name — to stop it.

Before the next irreversible decision, someone must have the authority to stop continuation — by name.
