Governance Gap

Most governance systems define approval.

RACI structures define responsibility. KPI systems optimize execution. Human-in-the-loop models introduce review and oversight.

But very few governance structures clearly define who, by name, can refuse irreversible continuation once institutional momentum already exists.

Human-In-The-Loop

A human may review the process without possessing meaningful interruption authority.

Distributed Responsibility

As responsibility spreads across systems and committees, refusal authority can disappear operationally.

Institutional Momentum

Once execution begins, continuation often becomes structurally easier than interruption.

Named Refusal Authority

A safeguard only becomes real when a specific individual can explicitly refuse continuation.

Decision Authority Research

A governance blind spot inside existing systems.

Decision Authority Research is an independent conceptual research project examining refusal authority, irreversible execution, and governance structure before continuation proceeds.

It is not a legal institution, regulatory body, or academic organization.

The work focuses on a single structural question:

Who, by name, is authorized to stop irreversible continuation?

Before the next irreversible decision, someone must have the authority to refuse continuation.
