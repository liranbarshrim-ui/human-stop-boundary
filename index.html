The Problem

Most organizations assign responsibility.
Few define who can stop.

Organizations are designed to move decisions forward. Approval paths, escalation chains, review committees — all built for continuation.

The harder function — who can interrupt a decision already in motion, on named authority, within the window that matters — is rarely defined before it is needed.

When something goes wrong, accountability does not stay distributed. It converges to a name. The question is whether that name was clear in advance, or assigned afterward.

Silent executive exposure is not the absence of responsibility.
It is the absence of a named stop.
