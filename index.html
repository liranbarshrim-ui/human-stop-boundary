A process is not controlled by the person who approves it.

It is controlled by the person whose refusal cannot be bypassed.
When organizations ask this question
The question is always relevant. Four moments make it urgent.
01Before an AI system is deployed into a decision it cannot easily be pulled back from.
02Before a release, launch, or go-live where rollback is expensive or impossible.
03After a near miss — when someone did say no, and the process continued anyway.
04After a regulatory finding that asks who was accountable — and the answers don't match.

In each of these moments, the cost of not knowing who holds binding refusal is no longer theoretical.

A narrower question

Governance reviews document controls, responsibilities, and decision structures.

DAR asks a narrower question:
Can a named person prevent continuation, and does that refusal bind the process?

What makes this difficult
Most organizations can answer these questions.

What is less clear is whether everyone gives the same answer.

Process owner
may identify one authority
Risk
may identify another
Legal

Each answer can appear reasonable in isolation. The ambiguity often becomes visible only when the answers are compared.

A two-minute test
Before requesting a review, choose one high-stakes process and answer three questions.
1 Who can stop it?
2 Can that refusal be overridden?
3 Who can authorize continuation after a stop?

If all three answers are named, documented, and immediately available, you may not need this review.

If one answer depends on interpretation, escalation, or who is asked, the question may not be fully resolved.
