Governance Authorization Diagnostic

Three names.
Do they exist?

Named refusal authority requires three distinct people. This diagnostic checks whether your organization has named all three — and whether continuation is authorized or proceeding by default.

Question 1 of 3
01 — Refusal Authority

Who, by name, holds the institutional right to refuse continuation of your most consequential automated decision the moment something unexpected appears?

Refusal authority is named in advance. Not a role. Not a committee. A person. Where no name exists, continuation is authorized by default.

A One specific person, named in writing, with documented refusal authority before execution proceeds.
B A senior leader would make the call, but refusal authority is not formally assigned. Authority exists in practice. Not in writing.
C It is unclear. Multiple people might be involved. No single name exists.
02 — Hold Authority

After refusal authority is invoked, who is responsible for ensuring execution remains suspended until a governance response arrives?

Hold authority is distinct from refusal authority. A refusal that no one owns becomes a refusal that dissolves. If maintaining the suspension requires convening, it has already failed.

A A named individual, different from whoever invoked refusal, holds the decision in suspension until governance responds.
B The same person who refused is also responsible for maintaining the suspension. Refusal and hold are not separated.
C There is no defined hold mechanism. The refusal would likely dissolve under institutional pressure.
03 — Authorization Authority

Who holds the documented right to authorize continuation — and is it a different person from whoever invoked refusal?

If the person who refuses can also authorize continuation, the refusal is a delay, not a governance event. Authorization must carry documented rationale and consequential personal exposure.

A A third named individual holds authorization authority, separate from both refusal and hold. Authorization requires written rationale.
B A senior person would authorize continuation, but it is not formally separated or documented.
C There is no defined authorization process. Continuation resumes when institutional pressure requires it.
