Focus

One recurring question drives this work:

Who is explicitly named, in advance, to stop a decision before it becomes irreversible?

Not a team. Not a process. Not a policy. A single individual — named, committed, and bound to refusal before execution begins.

Most organizations cannot answer this question. The absence of an answer is not a procedural gap. It is a structural choice — one that transfers accountability to whoever is left standing after the outcome.

The framework

The Decision Authority Record (DAR) emerged from this question. It is not a methodology or a product. It is a question that cannot be deferred:

When a decision becomes irreversible under uncertainty, someone must have the authority to stop it — by name.

DAR defines who that person is, in writing, before execution occurs.

Applies to

Boards. Chief Risk Officers. General Counsel. Heads of Compliance. Executives in fintech, clinical governance, aviation, critical infrastructure, and agentic AI — any domain where an irreversible decision can be reached without a named stop authority.

What this is not
  • Legal advice
  • A regulatory certification
  • A software product
  • A compliance framework
  • A substitute for internal governance
Why this matters now

As AI systems, automated workflows, and high-speed decisions scale, many organizations still cannot identify who is explicitly empowered to stop irreversible execution.

The question is not new. The consequences of leaving it unanswered are becoming harder to absorb.

When systems fail, responsibility resolves to a name.

The work begins before that moment.
