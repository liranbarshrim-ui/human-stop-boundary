Decision Authority Research

Liran Bar-Shrim

Independent researcher focused on accountability where complex systems move faster than ownership.

Built outside institutions around one unresolved question: who is explicitly empowered to stop an irreversible decision before it is too late?

Focus

Many organizations define how decisions move forward. Far fewer define who can truly stop them.

This work examines the structural gap between process approval and real human authority.

When no one is clearly named in advance, responsibility is often assigned afterward.

The Work

The research explores governance structures for boards, regulated sectors, critical operations, and AI systems where speed, automation, or complexity may outpace accountability.

It is especially relevant where decisions can become costly, public, or irreversible before ownership becomes clear.

Core Principle
When a decision becomes irreversible under uncertainty, someone must have authority to stop it — by name.
Why It Matters

Responsibility is often shared during success. It becomes personal during failure.

Clear authority before the event is usually stronger than explanation after it.
