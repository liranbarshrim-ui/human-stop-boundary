Liran Bar-Shrim focuses on decision authority under pressure.

His work centers on one recurring governance failure: critical decisions move forward, yet no one is clearly empowered to stop them in time.

Built independently and shaped by direct observation of how accountability behaves when consequences become personal.

Why This Perspective

Built through direct observation of how accountability behaves in the real world — especially under urgency, reputational pressure, and executive exposure.

Not based on committee language. Not based on internal politics. Based on what happens when leaders need clarity fast.

When consequences arrive, theory is late.

Focus

Many organizations define how decisions move forward. Far fewer define who can truly stop them.

This work examines the gap between process approval and real human authority.

When no one is clearly named in advance, responsibility is often assigned afterward.

Who This Helps
CEOs facing live decisions
Boards under scrutiny
General Counsel managing exposure
Risk leaders confronting escalation gaps
AI governance teams needing real human override authority
Core Principle
When a decision is already moving,
authority matters more than process.
Why It Matters

Responsibility is often shared during success. It becomes personal during failure.

Clear authority before the event is stronger than explanation after it.
