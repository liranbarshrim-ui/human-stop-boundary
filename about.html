Decision Authority Research

Liran Bar-Shrim

Independent advisor and researcher focused on decision authority, named stop authority, and irreversible decisions under uncertainty.

Focus

This work began with one recurring question:

Who is explicitly named, in advance, to stop a decision before it becomes irreversible?

Many systems define how decisions move forward. Far fewer define who can truly stop them.

The Work

The research explores practical governance structures for environments where speed, automation, and complexity can outpace human accountability.

It focuses on boards, regulated sectors, critical operations, and AI systems where unclear authority may become costly after failure.

Core Principle
When a decision becomes irreversible under uncertainty, someone must have authority to stop it — by name.
Why It Matters

Responsibility is often shared during success. It becomes personal during failure.

Clear authority before the event is usually stronger than explanation after it.
