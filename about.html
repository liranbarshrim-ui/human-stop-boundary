Liran Bar-Shrim

Liran Bar-Shrim focuses on stop authority. If no one is named in advance to stop a decision, the system does not actually know how to stop.

Built outside institutions. Focused on one question that most governance structures cannot answer by name.

Focus

Many organizations define how decisions move forward. Far fewer define who can truly stop them.

This work examines the structural gap between process approval and real human authority.

When no one is clearly named in advance, responsibility is often assigned afterward.

The Work

Advisory engagements for boards, regulated firms, clinical leadership, and AI governance leaders facing irreversible decisions under uncertainty.

Especially relevant where decisions can become costly, public, or irreversible before ownership becomes clear.

Core Principle
When a decision becomes irreversible under uncertainty, someone must have authority to stop it — by name.
Why It Matters

Responsibility is often shared during success. It becomes personal during failure.

Clear authority before the event is stronger than explanation after it.

Discuss a live governance gap, accountability exposure, or unclear stop authority.
