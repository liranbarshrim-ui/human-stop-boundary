for High-Stakes AI Decisions

DAR Executive Responsibility Charter

As organizations deploy AI into consequential workflows, speed of execution often exceeds speed of governance. Controls, policies, and approvals remain important. But when outcomes may become irreversible under uncertainty, governance requires something more specific: a named human authority with the power to stop execution in time.

01

Named Stop Authority

For each defined high-stakes AI process, one individual shall be identified in advance with authority to halt execution when material risk, uncertainty, or unexpected behavior emerges.

02

Separation of Powers

The authority to stop, the authority to release, and the authority to review shall not automatically reside in the same person.

03

Written Ownership

Any override, release, or continuation after a stop event requires documented ownership and rationale.

04

Human Signal Protection

Good-faith escalation by qualified personnel must be protected, reviewed rapidly, and never penalized merely for raising concern.

05

Real-World Kill Boundary

Where technically feasible, organizations shall maintain the ability to suspend the system's effect on external operations, customers, markets, or patients.
