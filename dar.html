Decision Accountability Review

Does refusal actually bind continuation?

A DAR review examines a claimed refusal authority at the point where a consequential process may become difficult or impossible to reverse. Naming the authority establishes an accountability claim; the boundary audit tests whether that claim actually binds the protected effect.

The scope

A structural review, not a universal control claim.

DAR does not claim that every system can be stopped by a human, that naming a person automatically gives them practical power, or that a framework can control arbitrary AI systems outside its enforcement boundary.

The review asks a narrower question: where a process is claimed to have refusal authority, can continuation proceed despite that refusal — and can the same protected effect be produced through another path?

The boundary matters.If the relevant action is outside the process or enforcement boundary being reviewed, DAR cannot establish a stop that the boundary does not actually govern.
The audit path

Name it. Define it. Bind it. Test it.

01

Name the authority

Identify the person, role, or authority claimed to be able to refuse continuation. Naming is evidence of accountability, not proof of power.

02

Define the stop condition

Identify the process, decision point, material uncertainty, and protected effect at which continuation is supposed to stop.

03

Bind the release

Identify who can release the stop, what exact target and effect that authority covers, and how the release is authenticated and protected against replay or widening.

04

Test alternate paths

Determine whether another principal, session, interface, mandate, recovery path, or internal route can produce the same effect without the required release.

A control point can influence a decision. A refusal authority can prevent continuation — if the boundary actually binds.
What DAR is not

Mirror, not mandate.

DAR does not decide whether an organization should stop. It does not certify an organization, assign an automated maturity score, or manufacture a finding from a predefined checklist.

It tests whether the claimed authority exists in the structure being reviewed and whether the evidence supports the claim.
