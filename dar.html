DAR — decision authority research

If this decision fails —
whose name will be evaluated against it?

Not a team. Not a process. One accountable person.

Now ask yourself:

Was that name defined before it moved?

When a decision becomes irreversible under uncertainty, someone must have the authority to stop it — by name.

A system can be technically safe and still proceed without anyone being required to stop it.

Exposure If an irreversible decision causes harm, responsibility will not remain abstract.

It will resolve to a name.

If no name was defined in advance, that name may be assigned after the fact.

This is not about whether a decision is correct.
It is about whether anyone was obligated to refuse it.

Real failures

Knight Capital. Boeing 737 MAX. Global outages. Market cascades.

Different industries. Same missing name.

See what happens when no one is named in time →

The longer this remains unnamed, the more likely it will be assigned under pressure.

What DAR does

One name. Before it moves.

DAR defines the single individual who holds stop authority over a specific irreversible decision pathway.

Not a role. Not a committee. One accountable individual — defined in advance.

If that authority does not exist, the decision is already moving — without anyone able to stop it.

Engagement deliverable
One critical decision pathway selected
One individual named in advance
One formal stop authority defined in writing
One Decision Authority Record issued
One written record of where stop authority is missing
Delivered within 7 to 10 business days

Advisory only. No system access required. No integration. This defines accountability before execution — not after failure.

This does not evaluate the system.
It defines who must stop it.

Why internal attempts fail

Most organizations can explain the decision.

They fail when asked who is personally able to stop it.

Because naming one person creates real exposure — and most systems are designed to avoid it.
