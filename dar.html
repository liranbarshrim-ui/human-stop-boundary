Executive Responsibility Charter

Named Stop Authority
for High-Stakes AI Decisions

As organizations deploy AI into consequential workflows, speed of execution often exceeds speed of governance. When outcomes may become irreversible under uncertainty, governance requires something more specific: a named human authority with the power to stop execution in time.

01

Named Stop Authority

One individual identified in advance with authority to halt execution when material risk or unexpected behavior emerges.

02

Separation of Powers

The authority to stop, release, and review shall not reside in the same person.

03

Written Ownership

Any override or continuation after a stop event requires documented ownership and rationale.

04

Human Signal Protection

Good-faith escalation must be protected and never penalized for raising concern.

05

Real-World Kill Boundary

Organizations shall maintain the ability to suspend the system's effect on external operations, customers, markets, or patients.

If something unexpected happens today, who by name can stop it in time?

If that answer is unclear, governance is incomplete.

Controls manage behavior.
Names carry consequences.
